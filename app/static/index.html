How to use the POPA Wharf Ops Console

This console is the single source of truth for berthing and dredging along the Port of Port Arthur wharf. It seeds itself from live AIS (real vessel positions), lets operators capture and place berth requests, and guards every booking against time × berth-space conflicts and controlling depth. Nothing here needs a spreadsheet. Below is what each part does.

1 The core idea — the wharf is a ruler, not a set of berths

The whole wharf face is measured linearly in feet along its centerline (“POPA stationing”). Every vessel and every dredge job is just a rectangle: a stretch of the wharf (a station range) over a stretch of time. A conflict is simply two rectangles that overlap in both time and space. “Berths” (Berth 1, 2, …) are just named handles for station ranges — convenient labels, not the real unit of allocation.

You’ll see two number systems on the map: canonical POPA station and the Dock No. feet painted on the quay (the yellow markers). Operators type positions in Dock No. feet — what you actually read off the wharf — and the system converts to canonical internally. The stern (larger Dock No.) is the low end; enter stern first, bow second.

2 The map

  • The chart is rotated so the wharf runs horizontally. The gold line is the measured quay centerline; yellow ticks are Dock No. stationing, red ticks POPA.
  • AIS contacts are dots coloured by ship type (see the legend, bottom-left). State is shown by motion, not colour: a solid dot is moored at our wharf, a hollow dot is stopped but off-berth (anchored/holding), and a pulsing ring means underway.
  • To-scale vessel outlines are drawn from the data. The Current / Planned toggle switches between live observed occupancy and the confirmed bookings at the timeline cursor’s moment.
  • A depth overlay paints the surveyed bed (shallow → deep ramp) with per-cell bed-elevation labels, so you can see where the channel shoals out.
  • Click a vessel row in the sidebar to locate its contact (a cyan ping on the chart).

3 The stats & conflict strip

The tiles at the top of the sidebar are live counters (vessels in the last 24 h, moored now, reservations, requests, confirmed, arrivals). Below them the Conflicts strip turns amber or red the moment any two placed rectangles overlap in time and space — the headline alert. It defaults to current/future and hides harbour service craft (tugs/pilots) so it stays a planning surface, not a log.

4 Sidebar tabs

  • Overview — the live picture: what’s alongside now, verification of planned arrivals against AIS, and depth-survey status.
  • Reservations — every booking. Create, edit, cancel, delete; assign a berth from the catalog; promote a row to Confirmed.
  • Berth requests — incoming requests (phone/email/operator entry, plus the optional AI channel). Each becomes a requested row you later place.
  • History — the full log of every booking and AIS-observed berthing. Use Filters to search by vessel, IMO, status, or date. Hover a row for a full ship dossier (record + reservation log + latest AIS fix).

5 Capturing a berth request

On the Berth requests tab, open New berth request and enter what the caller/emailer gave you (vessel, IMO, ETB/ETD, cargo, draft). Submitting lands the raw request for audit and creates a requested reservation with an unassigned station range — it doesn’t block anything yet, because placement is a deliberate operator step. You can Edit or Delete a request card later; edits re-project the linked reservation in place.

6 Placing & confirming a booking

  • Open a reservation’s Edit form to set its position. Assign a berth from the catalog (fills the station range for you) or type the Dock No. stern/bow feet directly.
  • Not sure where it fits? Use Find berth — the feasibility picker lists the discrete, vessel-sized candidate berths that are actually free in the window (free space minus everything occupying the wharf, padded by the 75 ft mooring gap, each depth-checked). Pick one and it’s placed.
  • Promote to Confirmed to lock it in. Two gates run automatically: a draft vs controlling-depth check (blocks with a 422 unless an operator overrides it to a warning) and the no-overlap constraint (a colliding confirmed booking surfaces as a 409). Only confirmed rows are hard-guarded; observed AIS rows are allowed to overlap on purpose — that overlap is a signal, not an error.

7 Vessel dimensions & the AIS override

For AIS-tracked ships (those with an MMSI) the AIS feed is authoritative on LOA/beam/draft — a manual edit to those is dropped so the feed can’t be clobbered. When AIS itself is wrong, tick “Override AIS dimensions” on the vessel edit form (or the red Manual override button on a request) to pin a corrected value; the ingestor then stops reverting it until you un-tick. Manual-only vessels (no MMSI) own all their dimensions normally.

8 The occupancy timeline (drawer under the map)

  • A Gantt chart: one lane per berth, bars coloured by status. Click the caret to expand it; drag its top edge to resize.
  • Use the window presets (±3d, Week, Next 7d/30d) and the pan arrows to scan time. Today recenters on now.
  • The NOW line is the current instant; the cursor (drag it, or press Play to sweep) drives the map’s Planned outlines — scrub time and watch the wharf fill and empty.

9 Verification — did reality match the plan?

The Overview tab checks your placements against observed AIS once vessels arrive: each planned row reads arrived / no-show / awaiting with a where-planned flag, and any unplanned berthing (a ship no request covered) is surfaced. This is read-only verification — AIS can’t position a not-yet-arrived ship, so it never places anything. Stale rows past their window auto-archive to completed/cancelled on evidence (a confirmed no-show is only flagged, never auto-cancelled).

10 Header & footer chrome

  • The « button collapses the sidebar; drag its right divider to resize.
  • The clock shows canonical Central Time over UTC — the whole system runs on America/Chicago.
  • The small dots by the clock are background-worker health (AIS ingest, occupancy, intake): green = healthy, amber = stale, red = error. Hover for detail.
  • The footer strip carries system status, refresh cadence, timezone, and the cursor’s lat/lon over the chart.
In one sentence: watch live AIS on the map and timeline, capture requests on the Berth requests tab, place them with Find berth, confirm them (depth + overlap gates protect you), and let verification tell you whether reality matched the plan.
